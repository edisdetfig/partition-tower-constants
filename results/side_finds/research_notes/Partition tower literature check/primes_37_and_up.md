# Partition congruences modulo primes ℓ ≥ 37: what the literature says (search done 12 Nov 2026)

Verdict key used below: **(a)** stated explicitly in print; **(b)** known in part or implicitly (what and where is given); **(c)** not found after the search listed under "Gaps". Notation: here ℓ is always the modulus and Q the auxiliary prime. Weaver, Johansson and Yang write *m* for the modulus and *ℓ* for the auxiliary prime, so their sentences are quoted with their letters. Quotes were taken from the downloaded full texts (arXiv HTML, or text pulled from the journal PDF). Formulas are written in plain text, and nothing was paraphrased inside quotation marks. The few residue checks marked "derived by hand" are simple arithmetic I did myself, not literature facts. Re-check them before use.

## 1. Are congruences for ℓ = 37, 41, 43, 47 at specific Q listed anywhere? (AAT's three accidents; 4049 and 8941 for ℓ = 43; 2999 for ℓ = 37)

### Takeaway
Only two printed sources give congruences modulo a prime ≥ 37 at named auxiliary primes. Both are for ℓ = 37 only:
- **Ahlgren–Allen–Tang (AAT)**, Type I at Q = 6599, 7541, 9547. The sign is printed only for 6599 and 7541.
- **Yang (IMRN 2011), Example 6.2.** This is a different family, p((37·Q^(2uk−1)n+1)/24) ≡ 0 (mod 37) for Q ≤ 61. Yang also prints a table of the two Hecke eigenvalues mod 37.

Atkin, Weaver and Johansson cover only 13 ≤ ℓ ≤ 31, and Ono and Chua give no explicit examples for ℓ ≥ 37. The primes 4049, 8941 (ℓ = 43) and 2999 (ℓ = 37) appear in no source found. Nothing was found for ℓ = 41, 43 or 47 at any specific Q.

### Cited Findings
**Ahlgren, Allen, Tang, "Congruences like Atkin's for the partition function".** arXiv:2112.09481, v1 17 Dec 2021, v2 18 Jul 2022. Published in Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. Ser. B 9 (2022), DOI 10.1090/btran/128; search metadata gives pp. 1044–1064. The PDF reads "Received by the editors December 17, 2021, and, in revised form, July 18, 2022."
- The ℓ ≥ 37 remark sits in §1, unnumbered, directly after the two open questions and before Theorem 1.2. Its wording is the same in arXiv v2 and in the published PDF (both checked). Verbatim: "Once ℓ ≥ 37 the spaces of modular forms which are relevant for congruences of Type I are no longer one-dimensional. However, one may still perform a search for accidents in the sense of Atkin. For example, when ℓ=37, the relevant space is two-dimensional. A computation of the Hecke eigenvalues of the two newforms in this space for Q<10000 yields three "accidents": there are three primes Q for which the Q th eigenvalue of each newform lies in the required residue class modulo a prime above ℓ in the field generated by its coefficients. In particular we have a congruence (1.6) when Q=6599, 7541, and 9547. For example, p((6599²·37n+1)/24) ≡ 0 (mod 37) if (n/6599) = −1, which leads to 3299 congruences modulo 37 of the form (1.5) with m=3. Similarly, we have p((7541²·37n+1)/24) ≡ 0 (mod 37) if (n/7541) = 1." — [AAT, arXiv HTML](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.09481); [AAT, published PDF](https://www.ams.org/journals/btran/2022-09-33/S2330-0000-2022-00128-X/S2330-0000-2022-00128-X.pdf)
- Neither version prints the sign for Q = 9547. Neither has a table of primes Q. Neither mentions ℓ = 41, 43 or 47. The numbers 2999, 4049 and 8941 do not occur anywhere in the full text (searched). — [AAT](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.09481)
- Definitions:
  - (1.5) is "p(Q³ℓn+β) ≡ 0 (mod ℓ)".
  - (1.6) is "p((Q²ℓn+1)/24) ≡ 0 (mod ℓ) if (n/Q) = ε_Q", called "Atkin I".
  - (1.7) is "For each of ℓ=5,7 and 13, Atkin [Atk68, Thm. 1, 2] showed that if Q ≡ −2 (mod ℓ) then p((Q²n+1)/24) ≡ 0 (mod ℓ) if (−n/ℓ) = −1 and (−n/Q) = −1. We will say that congruences (1.7) are of type "Atkin II." To the authors' knowledge, no examples of such congruences are known for ℓ ≥ 13."

  — [AAT](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.09481)
- Theorem 1.2: "Suppose that ℓ ≥ 5 is prime. Then a positive proportion of the primes Q ≡ 1 (mod ℓ) have the property that p((Q²ℓn+1)/24) ≡ 0 (mod ℓ) if (n/Q) = (−1/Q)^((ℓ−3)/2)." Theorems 1.3 and 1.4 cover primes Q ≡ −2 (mod ℓ):
  - Theorem 1.3 assumes "there exists an integer a with 2^a ≡ −1 (mod ℓ)".
  - Theorem 1.4 assumes 3^a ≡ −2 (mod ℓ), plus a hypothesis that no two newforms are congruent.

  — [AAT](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.09481)
- AAT on the earlier scope: "Let 13 ≤ ℓ ≤ 31 be prime. Atkin [Atk68, eq. (52)] gave examples of primes Q such that (1.6) …"; "Later, Weaver [Wea01] found more accidental eigenvalues for these primes"; "Johansson [Joh12] extended this list substantially; there are now more than 22 billion examples for primes ℓ ≤ 31." — [AAT](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.09481)

### Inferences
- **Background claim (AAT's three accidents for ℓ = 37, Q < 10,000):** verdict **(a)**. The sign is (a) for 6599 (−1) and for 7541 (+1). For 9547 the sign is **(c)**: it is not printed, so the project's −1 adds a fact.
  - Consistency check (derived by hand): 9547 = 258·37 + 1, so 9547 ≡ 1 (mod 37), and 9547 ≡ 3 (mod 4). The sign rule of AAT Theorem 1.2 then gives (n/Q) = (−1/Q)^17 = −1, which agrees with the project's −1.
  - 6599 ≡ 13 and 7541 ≡ 30 (mod 37) lie outside the Q ≡ 1 and Q ≡ −2 families of Theorems 1.2–1.4, so they are true "accidents" (derived by hand).
- **C7, ℓ = 43, Type I at Q = 4049 and 8941:** verdict **(c)**, not found anywhere. Derived by hand: 4049 ≡ 7 and 8941 ≡ 40 ≡ −3 (mod 43), outside AAT's Q ≡ 1 and Q ≡ −2 families. If correct, these would be new accidents for ℓ = 43, the first named congruences of this kind modulo 43 that I could find.
- **C7, ℓ = 37, "Type II" at Q = 2999:** the instance is **(c)**, not found. The criterion is **(a)**: Yang's Remark 3.2 with Theorem 7 says that when the T_{Q²} matrix vanishes mod m on the whole Hecke-invariant space, p((m^j Q³ n+1)/24) ≡ 0 (mod m) for Q ∤ n. Yang names this "the congruence appearing in Ono's theorem".
  - Yang's table has no Q ≤ 61 with both eigenvalues 0, which agrees with a first such Q being much larger.
  - 2999 ≡ 2 (mod 37) (derived by hand), so it is not one of Ono/Ahlgren–Ono's Q ≡ −1 (mod 24ℓ) primes. It would be an "accidental" Ono-type prime.
- **Name clash:** the project's "Type II" (both eigenvalues ≡ 0, Q³ form) is not AAT's "Type II". AAT's "Atkin II" is family (1.7): Q ≡ −2 (mod ℓ), with conditions on (−n/ℓ) and (−n/Q). To avoid confusion, call the project's family "Ono-type at an accidental zero". In ABR's (1.2) counting it gives progressions with modulus ℓQ⁴ (m = 4), while Type I gives m = 3.
- **C7, "none for ℓ = 41 and 47 below 10,000":** verdict **(c)**. No published search for these ℓ was found, so nothing confirms or contradicts it.
- **All explicit congruences modulo a prime ≥ 37 found in print:**
  - AAT: ℓ = 37, Type I, Q = 6599, 7541, 9547.
  - Yang: ℓ = 37, Q^(2uk−1) family for Q ≤ 61.
  - Boylan–Webb: along powers of ℓ only, for ℓ = 37, 41, 53, 89, 137, 1297.

## 2. Is the pair of conjugate newforms over a real quadratic field (ℓ ≥ 37) described in print?

### Takeaway
In part, and only for ℓ = 37. AAT and Yang both state that the relevant space is two-dimensional. Yang says its two eigenforms "are defined over a certain real quadratic number field", and that their reductions mod 37 are defined over F_37, which implies that 37 splits. Neither names the field. For 41, 43 and 47 nothing specific is printed beyond general dimension and rank results that give 2. The Atkin–Lehner sign class is printed (AAT; Yang/Guo–Ono). The project's defining polynomials (y² − y − 7187088385710, etc.) were not found.

### Cited Findings
- AAT §2.1, from Yang's Nagoya paper [Yan14]:
  - "(2.9) Sh_t : S_k(1, ν_η^r) ⟶ S^new_{2k−1}(6, −(8/r), −(12/r)) ⊗ (12/•)"
  - "Moreover, for all primes Q ≥ 5 we have (2.10) Sh_t(f|T(Q²)) = (Sh_t f)|T(Q)"
  - "there is a modular form f_ℓ ∈ S_{(ℓ−2)/2}(1, ν_η^{−ℓ}, Z) with (2.11) f_ℓ ≡ Σ p((ℓn+1)/24) q^{n/24} (mod ℓ)"
  - "each Shimura lift of f_ℓ is in the space S^new_{ℓ−3}(6, −(8/−ℓ), −(12/−ℓ)) ⊗ (12/•)"

  — [AAT](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.09481)
- AAT's ℓ = 37 remark (quoted in §1) speaks of "the two newforms in this space" and of "a prime above ℓ in the field generated by its coefficients". It does not name the field. — [AAT](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.09481)
- AAT's remark after Theorem 1.4: "a computation shows that there is no congruence modulo any prime above ℓ between distinct newforms in S^new_{ℓ−3}(6, −(8/−ℓ), −(12/−ℓ)) for any ℓ < 150. However it seems very difficult to remove this condition. For example when ℓ=71, there is such a congruence between two newforms in the space S^new_{ℓ−3}(6, (8/−ℓ), (12/−ℓ)). In particular, this space contains a Galois orbit consisting of three newforms defined over a field which is ramified at 71, and this orbit gives rise to only two distinct reductions modulo the prime above 71." Note that the ℓ = 71 example is in the opposite sign class. — [AAT](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.09481)
- Yang, after Corollary 3, for m ∈ {13,…,31}, citing Guo–Ono, IMRN 1999, Prop. 6: "the image of g_m under the Shimura correspondence is G_m ⊗ χ_12, where G_m is the unique normalized newform of weight m−3 on Γ_0(6) whose eigenvalues for the Atkin-Lehner involutions W_2 and W_3 are −(2/m) and −(3/m), respectively." — [Yang](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0904.2530)
- Yang, Remark 3.1: "(8) dim S_{r_m,(m−r_m−2)/2} = ⌊m/12⌋ − ⌊m/24⌋." Here S_{r,s} = {η(24τ)^r f(24τ) : f ∈ M_s(SL(2,Z))}, and Theorem 2 proves it is invariant under all T_{ℓ²}. — [Yang](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0904.2530)
- Yang, Example 6.2: "Let m=37. By Corollary 4, we know that F_{37,1} is congruent to a cusp form in S_{11,12} modulo 37. In fact, according to [8, Table 3.1], F_{37,1} ≡ η(24τ)^11 (E_4(24τ)³ + 17Δ(24τ)) mod 37. The two eigenforms of S_{11,12} are defined over a certain real quadratic number field, but the reduction of S_{11,12} ∩ Z[[q]] modulo 37 has eigenforms defined over F_37. They are f_1 = η(24τ)^11 (E_4(24τ)³ + 24Δ(24τ)), f_2 = η(24τ)^11 Δ(24τ)." The table of eigenvalues is in §1 above. Reference [8] is Chua 2003. — [Yang](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0904.2530)
- Boylan–Webb: the rank bound is R_ℓ = ⌊(ℓ+12)/24⌋. Their computations found: "For all primes ℓ ≤ 1297 with the exception of ℓ = 607, our calculations show that r_ℓ(1) = R_ℓ, and hence, that r_ℓ(m) = R_ℓ for all m ≥ 1." — [Boylan–Webb](https://www.ams.org/journals/tran/2013-365-04/S0002-9947-2012-05702-3/S0002-9947-2012-05702-3.pdf)

### Inferences
- **"The relevant space is two-dimensional":**
  - ℓ = 37: verdict **(a)** (AAT; Yang).
  - ℓ = 41, 43, 47: **(a)** through general results only. Yang's formula gives ⌊ℓ/12⌋ − ⌊ℓ/24⌋ = 3 − 1 = 2 (derived), and Boylan–Webb computed rank = R_ℓ = 2 for every ℓ ≤ 1297 except 607. No paper discusses these ℓ by name.
- **"One Galois-conjugate pair over a real quadratic field":**
  - ℓ = 37: **(a)** that the field is real quadratic (Yang, stated for the half-integral-weight eigenforms, which match the weight-34 newforms through (2.9)). The field is never named.
  - ℓ = 41, 43, 47: **(c)**.
- **Splitting of ℓ:**
  - "37 splits": **(b)**, implicit in Yang. He gives two distinct eigenforms defined over F_37, with different eigenvalue rows. If 37 were inert, the reductions would be conjugate over F_{37²}. If 37 were ramified, both conjugates would reduce to the same form. (This is my inference.)
  - 41 and 43 split, 47 inert: **(c)**.
  - AAT's computed fact that no two distinct newforms in this sign class are congruent for ℓ < 150 fits the project's picture of two twins with different reductions.
- **Matching the project's objects to print:**
  - Yang's S_{11,12} has weight 11/2 + 12 = 35/2 = (37−2)/2, so it is AAT's f_ℓ-space for ℓ = 37 (derived).
  - The project's "Atkin–Lehner sign class of the tower" should be AAT's (−(8/−ℓ), −(12/−ℓ)), which equals Yang/Guo–Ono's (−(2/ℓ), −(3/ℓ)) for prime ℓ.
- **The quadratic polynomials** (37: y² − y − 7187088385710; 41: y² − y − 62801230922892; 43: y² − y − 3231324102; 47: y² − y − 118780096383848578140): verdict **(c)**, not found in any source searched.

### Gaps

## 3. The structure of p(ℓ^b n + δ) along powers of ℓ for ℓ ≥ 37: rank 2, the two-term recurrence, ℓ-adic constants

### Takeaway
The rank-2 structure is in print, in general form and with examples:
- **The rank bound.** FKO give ⌊(ℓ−1)/12⌋ − ⌊(ℓ²−1)/(24ℓ)⌋. Boylan–Webb state that this equals ⌊(ℓ+12)/24⌋ and use that form. It is 2 for 37 ≤ ℓ ≤ 59.
- **Computed ranks and relations.** Boylan–Webb computed that the rank equals this bound for every prime ≤ 1297 except 607. They print three-term relations for ℓ = 53, mod 53 and mod 53² (the mod-53² relation on levels 3, 5, 7), and periods c_37 = 36 and c_41 = 10.
- **Hecke structure.** Yang proved Chua's conjecture: the tower stays in the two-dimensional Hecke-invariant space, where U_{m²} ≡ T_{m²} (mod m).
- **Not found:** the explicit constants T, N for 37–47, their identification as the trace and norm of (3|ℓ)a_ℓ over the pair, and per-twin ℓ-adic constants.

## 4. Is the two-step law along Q (from T(Q²)² − t·T(Q²) + n = 0) stated anywhere for p(n)?

### Takeaway
Not in the project's scalar form with trace and norm. The equivalent matrix form is in print: Yang (IMRN 2011), Corollary 6, gives the exact recursion for f|U_{Q²}^k on the t-dimensional Hecke-invariant space. It uses a block companion matrix [[A, −Q^(r+2s−2) I],[I, 0]]. For the partition space this is −Q^(m−4) ≡ −Q^(−3) (mod m), the same Q^(−3) term as in the project's law. Remark 5.1 gives the per-eigenform 2×2 matrices [[a_Q^(i), −Q^(m−4)],[1, 0]]. Adding Cayley–Hamilton for t = 2 and the Hecke formula (AAT (2.5)) gives the project's law, but nobody writes it out. In rank 1 (13 ≤ ℓ ≤ 31) the corresponding one-constant law along Q is explicit (FKO Corollary 1.4; AAT Lemmas 5.1–5.2).

### Cited Findings
