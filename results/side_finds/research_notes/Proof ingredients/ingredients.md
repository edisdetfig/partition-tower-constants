# Proof ingredients: what the published sources state (12 Nov 2026)

Purpose: collect, word for word where short, the published statements behind our measured claim
α_f = (3|ℓ)·u_f(−2) for the operator that moves the tower's limit two levels up. No proof is written here.

How the sources were read. PDFs were turned into text with `pdftotext`. Where it dropped the Greek letters,
they were restored from the TeX font glyph codes; for arXiv papers the TeX source was read directly.
Each quote is verbatim and under 125 characters. Tags:
- **[read]**: I read it in the source named.
- **[derived]**: a short consequence I worked out from the quoted statements. It is not in the source.
- **[unverified]**: known only second-hand, or not read.

Notation: H_b(n) = p(ℓ^b n + δ_b) with 24δ_b ≡ 1 (mod ℓ^b), 0 < δ_b < ℓ^b. χ12 = (12/·) is the character of
conductor 12.

## 1. The tower as an operator

### 1a. Folsom–Kent–Ono, main text [read]
Source: A. Folsom, Z. A. Kent, K. Ono, "ℓ-adic properties of the partition function", Adv. Math. 229 (2012)
1586–1609. I read the authors' posted copy: https://uva.theopenscholar.com/files/ken-ono/files/133_8.pdf.
That copy is a preprint (its ref. [12] still calls Boylan–Webb "accepted"). The journal version was not
compared [unverified].

- (1.1) P_ℓ(b; z) := Σ_{n≥0} p((ℓ^b n + 1)/24) q^{n/24}, with "p(α) = 0 if α < 0 or α ∉ ℤ".
- (2.1) Φ_ℓ(z) := η(ℓ²z)/η(z).
- (2.2) Atkin's operator: "(Σ a(n)q^n) | U(ℓ) := Σ a(nℓ)q^n".
- (2.3) "f(z) | D(ℓ) := (Φ_ℓ(z) · f(z)) | U(ℓ)".
- (2.4)–(2.5) L_ℓ(0; z) := 1. For b ≥ 1: L_ℓ(b; z) := L_ℓ(b−1; z)|U(ℓ) if b is even, and L_ℓ(b−1; z)|D(ℓ) if b is
  odd.
- Lemma 2.1: "L_ℓ(b; z) = η(z) · P_ℓ(b; z) if b is even, η(ℓz) · P_ℓ(b; z) if b is odd."
- Lemma 2.2: "If b is a nonnegative integer, then L_ℓ(b; z) is in M_0^!(Γ0(ℓ)) ∩ ℤ[[q]]." Also: "if b ≥ 1, then
  L_ℓ(b; z) vanishes at i∞, and its only pole is at the cusp at 0."
- The main-text space is therefore weight-0 weakly holomorphic functions on Γ0(ℓ) with integer coefficients.
  Modulo ℓ^m these are matched to level-1 cusp forms of weight k_ℓ(m) := ℓ^{m−1}(ℓ−1). Theorem 4.3: "L_ℓ(b; z) is
  in Ω_ℓ(m)" for b ≥ b_ℓ(m).
- (1.2) b_ℓ(m) := 2(⌊(ℓ−1)/12⌋ + 2)m − 3.
- §3.3: Ω_ℓ(m) is the ℤ/ℓ^mℤ-module generated by the images G_ℓ(b; z) (mod ℓ^m), b ≥ b_ℓ(m), of forms G that are
  "good for (ℓ,m)".
- Theorem 1.2: "If ℓ ≥ 5 is prime and m ≥ 1, then Ω_ℓ(m) is a ℤ/ℓ^mℤ-module with rank ≤ ⌊(ℓ−1)/12⌋."
  For b ≥ b_ℓ(m) it gives P_ℓ(b; z) ≡ F_ℓ(b; z)/η(z) (b even) and F_ℓ(b; z)/η(ℓz) (b odd) (mod ℓ^m), with
  F_ℓ(b; z) ∈ Ω_ℓ(m). The abstract's sharper count: "at most ⌊(ℓ−1)/12⌋ − ⌊(ℓ²−1)/24ℓ⌋ many special q-series".
- Theorem 1.3: "If 5 ≤ ℓ ≤ 31 and m ≥ 1, then for b ≥ b_ℓ(m) we have that P_ℓ(b;24z) (mod ℓ^m)" "is an
  eigenform of all of the weight k_ℓ(m) − 1/2 Hecke operators on Γ0(576)."
- Theorem 5.1 (Ahlgren–Boylan): P_ℓ(m; 24z) = Σ p(ℓ^m n + δ_ℓ(m)) q^{24n+r_ℓ(m)} ≡ η(24z)^{r_ℓ(m)} F(k⁺_ℓ(m); 24z)
  (mod ℓ^m), where r_ℓ(m) := (24δ_ℓ(m) − 1)/ℓ^m.
- Theorem 5.2 (Garvan and Yang): for odd 0 < r < 24 and even s ≥ 0, S_{r,s} := {η(24z)^r f(24z) : f ∈ M_s} "is
  a Hecke invariant subspace of S_{s+r/2}(Γ0(576), χ12)".

Normalization against H_b [derived from (1.1) and Lemma 2.1]:
- P_ℓ(b; 24z) = Σ_n H_b(n) q^{24n + r}, where r = r_ℓ(b) = (24δ_b − 1)/ℓ^b and 0 < r < 24.
- r ≡ −ℓ^{−b} (mod 24). So r = 23 for even b, and r ≡ −ℓ (mod 24) for odd b.
- Hence L_ℓ(b; z) = q^{(1+r)/24}·Π(1−q^n)·Σ H_b(n)q^n for even b, and q^{(ℓ+r)/24}·Π(1−q^{ℓn})·Σ H_b(n)q^n for
  odd b.

The two-level step [derived]: U(ℓ)∘U(ℓ) = U(ℓ²) on q-series. So for even b, L(b+2) = (Φ_ℓ·L(b))|U(ℓ²), and on
the generating functions P_ℓ(b+2; 24z) = P_ℓ(b; 24z)|U(ℓ²), where U(ℓ²): Σ a(n)q^n ↦ Σ a(ℓ²n)q^n. On weight-0
functions the step is therefore U(ℓ²) conjugated by a fixed factor: η(z) for even b, η(ℓz) for odd b.

### 1b. Boylan–Webb [read]
Source: M. Boylan, J. J. Webb, "The partition function modulo prime powers", Trans. AMS 365 (2013) 2169–2206,
DOI 10.1090/S0002-9947-2012-05702-3. Reprint: https://people.math.sc.edu/boylan/reprints/partition.pdf.
No arXiv version was found.

- (1.8) "f(z) | X(ℓ) := f(z) | U(ℓ) | D(ℓ)" and (1.9) "f(z) | Y(ℓ) := f(z) | D(ℓ) | U(ℓ)".
- Ω_odd(b, m) = Span{L_ℓ(b; z)|X(ℓ)^s} and Ω_even(b, m) = Span{L_ℓ(b; z)|Y(ℓ)^t}. So X(ℓ) is the two-level step on
  odd b, and Y(ℓ) = (multiply by Φ_ℓ, then U(ℓ²)) is the step on even b.
- Theorem 1.4 (ℓ ≥ 5, m ≥ 1, assuming d_ℓ = 0) has two parts:
  - b_ℓ(m) ≤ 2m − 1, and the nested modules Ω_odd(1,m) ⊆ Ω_odd(3,m) ⊆ … become constant once 2b+1 ≥ b_ℓ(m).
  - The stable module has rank r_ℓ(m) ≤ R_ℓ := ⌊(ℓ+12)/24⌋. The same holds for the even modules.
- Remark 1 says these maps are isomorphisms: "X(ℓ) : Ω_odd(m) → Ω_odd(m), Y(ℓ) : Ω_even(m) → Ω_even(m)". So are
  U(ℓ): Ω_odd → Ω_even and D(ℓ): Ω_even → Ω_odd.
- Remark 2: Theorem 7.1 of the FKO appendix generalizes stability and finiteness, but bounds "require explicit
  analysis specific to the inputs (1.5)–(1.10)".
- §1: "Calculations reveal, for all primes 5 ≤ ℓ ≤ 1300 (the primes we considered), that d_ℓ = 0."
- (6.5): L(b + 2r; z) ≡ c_0 L(b; z) + … + c_{r−1} L(b + 2(r−1); z) (mod ℓ^m). Also: "the coefficients are
  independent of b". This is a fixed characteristic relation for X(ℓ) modulo ℓ^m.
- **§6.1 reports an exception.** "For all primes ℓ ≤ 1297 with the exception of ℓ = 607, our calculations show
  that r_ℓ(1) = R_ℓ". For ℓ = 607 it says: "r_607(m) = R_607 − 1 for all m ≥ 1."
  - R_607 = 25, from (1.13) [derived].
  - A sentence in §6.2 reads "s = R_607 − 2 = 24", which does not fit R_607 = 25. My copy may be garbled; I used the
    §6.1 statement [unverified which is right].

### 1c. FKO Appendix (by N. Ramsey; the proof is due to Calegari) [read]
- Theorem 7.1, quoted in pieces:
  - "Fix a prime ℓ, an odd integer k, a positive integer N, and a Dirichlet character χ modulo 4N."
  - "There exists a module Ω ⊆ ℤ_ℓ[χ][[q]] of finite rank over ℤ_ℓ[χ]" that is preserved by "all
    half-integral weight Hecke operators".
  - F = Σ a(n)q^n is "a classical modular form that is holomorphic away from the cusps". L is a number field holding
    its coefficients and the values of χ, embedded in Q̄_ℓ.
  - "Then for all integers m and s sufficiently large, there is a congruence": Σ a(ℓ^{2s}n)q^n ≡ ω_s (mod ℓ^m) for
    some ω_s ∈ Ω ⊗_{ℤ_ℓ[χ]} O_{L,p}.
  - The theorem itself does not name the weight or level of F. §7.1 takes F of "weight k/2, level 4Nℓ^s, and
    nebentypus χ".
- Remark (1): "given ℓ, k, N, and χ, the module Ω can be explicitly computed."
- Remark (3): "If the rank of Ω is 1, then since Ω is Hecke-stable, it is spanned by a single nonzero Hecke
  eigenform." It continues: "the images of F under sufficiently many iterates of U(ℓ²) are congruent modulo ℓ^m to
  multiples of this single form".
- §7.1 setting:
  - Spaces: Ramsey's Banach spaces M_{k/2}(4N, K, r) of r-overconvergent forms. These come from Ramsey, Ann. Inst.
    Fourier 56 (2006), and "The half-integral weight eigencurve", Algebra Number Theory 2 (2008).
  - Level: "One should think of 4N as the ℓ-adic 'tame level'", with ℓ ∤ 4N. The exposition takes ℓ ≠ 2.
  - Weights: general ℓ-adic weights κ absorb the ℓ-part χ_ℓ of the character.
  - Operator: (7.1) U(ℓ²): Σ a(n)q^n ↦ Σ a(ℓ²n)q^n at the cusps over ∞ on X0(ℓ), and "the operator U(ℓ²) is
    compact". Then: "Taking σ = 0, we see that the space of ordinary overconvergent forms is finite-dimensional."
- Poles: "U(ℓ²) strictly reduces the order of any poles", so U(ℓ²)^t F is a genuine ℓ-adic form for large t.
- §7.2 proof:
  - "e : F ↦ lim_{s→∞} U(ℓ²)^{s!} F" is the projector onto the ordinary subspace.
  - "there exists a positive an integer t such that" lim_k U^{k!+t}(ℓ²)(F) ∈ M†_κ(4N, K, χ_tm)^ord.
  - Ω is the set of integral q-expansions in M†_κ(4N, Q_ℓ(χ_tm, κ), χ_tm)^ord.
- What it proves: the limits **lie in** the finite slope-0 (ordinary) space, and Ω is the integral part of that
  whole space. It does **not** state that the tower's limits span Ω.
- How FKO's tower fits [derived]:
  - Even b: F = η(24z)^{−1} (weight −1/2, Γ0(576), χ12), and P_ℓ(b; 24z) = F|U(ℓ²)^{b/2}.
  - Odd b: F = η(24z)^{−1}|U(ℓ) (level 576ℓ).
  - Both are Theorem 7.1's Σ a(ℓ^{2s}n)q^n.

### 1d. Calegari, AWS 2013 notes, §4.5–4.7 [read]
Source: F. Calegari, "Congruences between modular forms", Arizona Winter School 2013 lecture notes:
https://swc-math.github.io/aws/2013/2013CalegariLectureNotes.pdf.

- 4.5: for half-integral weights "the appropriate operator at p is U_{p²}".
- 4.5.1 Theorem (Ramsey): "Let k be a half-integer. There exists a space of overconvergent forms M†_k(Γ, r) of
  weight k," "and U_{p²} acts compactly on this space."
- 4.6.1:
  - "Let η̃^{−1} denote the projection of η^{−1} to the ordinary subspace."
  - Then U^n η^{−1} ≡ U^n η̃^{−1} mod p^{n−c}, for "a constant c depending only on p".
  - Also: "this has no content if p = 2 or p = 3".
  - And: "The form η^{−1} is meromorphic of weight −1/2. The form η^{−1}(24τ) has level Γ0(576) and character χ".
- 4.6.2 defines forms of "η-level one" by f(γτ)/f(τ) = η(γτ)^k/η(τ)^k. Then "U_{p²} η^{−1} ∈ S†_{−1/2}(1, r)". In
  the usual normalization: "U_{p²} η^{−1}(24τ) ∈ S†_{−1/2}(Γ0(576), r)".
- He writes F = U_{p²}η^{−1}(24τ) = e_p F + H + H_{≥1}, "where H is a finite sum of generalized eigenforms of slope
  strictly between 0 and 1". Then: "It suffices to show that H is zero."
- 4.6.3: "By the overconvergent Shimura correspondence [Ram09], there exists a corresponding overconvergent
  eigenform of weight −2". Also: "A priori, one might expect the level to be Γ0(288). However, after twisting, this
  form lies in Γ0(6)".
- **4.6.4 Lemma.**
  - "The image of the Shimura correspondence from S_{−1/2}(1, r) lies in S†_{−2}(Γ0(6), r) ⊗ χ12,"
  - "where χ12 is the quadratic character of conductor 12."
  - "Moreover, the eigenvalues of the corresponding form of level Γ0(6) of U2 and U3 are given by 2^{−2} and 3^{−2}"
- Proof status: none is given. He writes: "One can provide fairly soft proofs of these type of facts using the
  trace formula". One would show "the appropriate spaces have the same traces of (U_{p²})^n and (U_p)^n".
- 4.7, p = 5:
  - U_{p²} slopes on S†_{−1/2}(1, r): "2, 7, 9, 15, 19, 22, 27, 29, 36, 39, . . .".
  - Σ p((25^m n + 1)/24) q^n ≡ λ_1^m·φ_1 + O(5^{7m}).
  - "φ1 is an eigenform for the Hecke operators T_{ℓ²} with ℓ ≠ 5 as well as the operator U_25".
- 4.7.1 Exercise (⋆), p = 5: the weight −2, level 6 forms "new at 2 and 3 with w2 = w3 = +1".

**Answer to ingredient 1.**
- The operator:
  - In half-integral weight, it is Atkin's U(ℓ²) on P_ℓ(b; 24z): FKO App. (7.1), Calegari's U_{p²}.
  - On weight-0 functions on Γ0(ℓ), it is "U(ℓ) composed with multiplication by Φ_ℓ = η(ℓ²z)/η(z)": BW's
    X(ℓ) = U(ℓ) then D(ℓ) (odd b) and Y(ℓ) = D(ℓ) then U(ℓ) (even b).
- The spaces:
  - FKO main text: M_0^!(Γ0(ℓ)) ∩ ℤ[[q]] modulo ℓ^m.
  - FKO appendix: overconvergent half-integral weight κ of tame level 4N = 576, at the weight of η^{−1}.
  - Calegari: S†_{−1/2} of "η-level one", or Γ0(576) in the usual normalization.
- The appendix proves that the limit lies in the slope-0 part. It also proves that rank 1 gives a single
  eigenform.

**Gap for our use:**
- No source states that the tower's limit spans the whole ordinary space. BW's computation at ℓ = 607 (rank R − 1)
  suggests that it can fail.
- Theorem 7.1 only gives existence modulo ℓ^m. It says nothing about eigenvalues.
- Calegari's 4.6.1 rests on Lemma 4.6.4, which has no proof.

## 2. Ramsey's ℓ-adic (overconvergent) Shimura lift

Source: N. Ramsey, "The overconvergent Shimura lifting", IMRN 2009, no. 2, 193–220; arXiv:0906.3255. I read the TeX
source; theorem numbers in the IMRN version were not checked [read].

- Abstract: "We construct a rigid-analytic map from the the author's half-integral weight cuspidal eigencurve". It
  goes "to its integral weight counterpart that interpolates the classical Shimura lifting."
- Hypotheses:
  - p is an odd prime and p ∤ N.
  - Half-integral side: tame level 4N, cuspidal eigencurve D̃ (Ramsey, Algebra Number Theory 2008).
  - Integral side: tame level 2N, cuspidal eigencurve D (Buzzard).
  - "For the remainder of the paper all spaces of modular forms of half-integral weight will be taken at tame level
    4N".
- Weights:
  - κ ∈ 𝒲 (continuous characters of ℤ_p^×) factors as κ = τ^i κ', with τ the Teichmüller character.
  - The classical weight k/2 with λ = (k−1)/2 embeds at weight λτ^j.
  - Downstairs the map is 𝟐(κ) = κ².
  - So weight −1/2 (λ = −1) goes to integral weight −2 [derived].
- Hecke operators:
  - Upstairs: T_{ℓ²} (ℓ ∤ 4Np), U_{ℓ²} (ℓ | 4Np) and ⟨d⟩_{4N}.
  - Downstairs: T_ℓ, U_ℓ and ⟨d⟩_{2N}.
  - The algebra map in §5 is T_ℓ ↦ T_{ℓ²}, U_ℓ ↦ U_{ℓ²}, ⟨d⟩_{2N} ↦ ⟨d²⟩_{4N}. This includes U_p ↦ U_{p²}.
- Main theorem (§5): "There exists a unique diagram" D̃_red → D_red, Z̃_red → Z_red, 𝒲 → 𝒲. The maps "are
  characterized on points by 𝟐(κ) = κ² and g(κ, α) = (κ², α)".
  - Here α is the reciprocal of the U_{p²}-eigenvalue upstairs (U_p downstairs).
  - So the U_{p²}-eigenvalue of a half-integral eigen-system **equals** the U_p-eigenvalue of its lift. There is no
    twist at p in Ramsey's normalization [derived from the definitions].
  - Sh is fixed by classical points: on a nonzero classical F ∈ S̃^cl_{k/2}(4Np, K, χτ^j) with k ≥ 5, Sh gives the
    classical Shimura lift.
- Classical input (his Theorem 1, Shimura–Niwa–Cipra):
  - "Let F be a nonzero holomorphic cusp form of level 4N, weight k/2 ≥ 5/2 and nebentypus χ."
  - The lift f has "weight k − 1, level 2N, and character χ²".
  - It "is an eigenform for all T_ℓ and U_ℓ with eigenvalues α_ℓ".
- §6, image and fibres:
  - "The map Sh carries D̃^i_red isomorphically onto a union of irreducible components of D^{2i}_red."
  - 𝒲^i and 𝒲^{i'}, with i' = i + (p−1)/2, both map onto 𝒲^{2i}, and "the map Sh is everywhere two-to-one".
  - The link is the operator U_p of his Proposition in §6: "There is a compact O(X)-linear map U_p" from weight
    ε(X) to weight X, where ε(κ) = κτ^{(p−1)/2}.
  - It has "the effect Σ a_n q^n ↦ Σ a_{pn} q^n" and commutes with T_{ℓ²} and U_{ℓ²}.
  - It satisfies U_p∘⟨d⟩_{4N} = (−1/d)^{(p−1)/2}⟨d⟩_{4N}∘U_p.
- Control theorem (§2): an F with P(U_{p²})F = 0, where every root of P has valuation < 2λ − 1, is classical.
  - Only classical forms of level 4Np are counted as classical.
  - Remark: forms of level 4Np^m are omitted "because we have not proven an analog of our control theorem".
- How our two parities fit [derived]:
  - Even-b towers sit at weight −1/2 with tame character χ12.
  - Odd-b towers sit at weight −1/2 twisted by τ^{(ℓ−1)/2}, with tame character χ12·χ_{−4}^{(ℓ−1)/2}.
  - These are the two components over one integral component. FKO's U(ℓ) between them is Ramsey's U_p.
- Follow-ups:
  - Longo–Nicole, Doc. Math. 18 (2013), arXiv:1210.6739, say Ramsey "extended the Shimura ... lifting to the
    overconvergent setting" [read].
  - Hida, "On Λ-adic forms of half-integral weight for SL(2)/ℚ", Number Theory (Paris 1992–93), LMS Lect. Note Ser.
    (1995): a Λ-adic Shimura lift for ordinary forms [unverified, not read].
  - Stevens, "Λ-adic modular forms of half-integral weight and a Λ-adic Shintani lifting", Contemp. Math. 174 (1994)
    [unverified].
  - Park, Manuscripta Math. 131 (2010), overconvergent Shintani lift [unverified].
- Calegari's Lemma 4.6.4 is quoted in full in 1d. Hypotheses: p > 3; source space S_{−1/2}(1, r) (η-level one);
  target S†_{−2}(Γ0(6), r) ⊗ χ12. No proof is given.

**Gap for our use:**
- Sh maps systems of eigenvalues on reduced cuspidal eigencurves. It says nothing about the dimension of eigenspaces
  (multiplicities). It is not a Hecke-module isomorphism.
- It is cuspidal. The tower starts from the meromorphic η(24z)^{−1}. Calegari asserts S†, while FKO's appendix
  only gets M†.
- It lands at tame level 2N = 288 with trivial character. The step down to Γ0(6) ⊗ χ12 with U2 = 2^{−2},
  U3 = 3^{−2} is only Calegari's unproved Lemma 4.6.4.

## 3. Hida's control at a non-classical weight, and ordinary ⇒ overconvergent

- **Hida, "p-adic automorphic forms on reductive groups", Astérisque 298 (2005) 147–254** [read].
  - I read the author's version, https://www.math.ucla.edu/~hida/Poincare.pdf (IHP lectures, 2000). The numbering in
    the published version was not checked.
  - The setting (§3.1) is V, Katz's p-adic modular forms on the Igusa tower, tensored with T_p = ℚ_p/ℤ_p.
  - "e = lim_{n→∞} U(p)^{n!}"; "V^ord = eV has Pontryagin dual which is projective over W[[G_m(ℤ_p)]]".
  - "For any k ≥ 3, there is a canonical isomorphism V^ord[−k] ≅ eH^0(S, ω^k ⊗ T_p)".
  - The argument before Theorem 3.1: "If (F) holds for one k, it holds for all κ inducing χ". The specialization at
    κ is free of rank s "because its dual V[κ] is p-divisible". Also: π "induces an isomorphism modulo (1 + X) − γ^κ
    for all κ inducing χ".
  - Theorem 3.1: "Suppose that (F) holds for one k." "Then V_ord[χ] is Λ-free of finite rank s(χ)". Also,
    V_ord ⊗_{W[[ℤ_p^×]],k} W ≅ Hom_W(G^ord_k(Γ1(N); W), W) when (3.1) holds for k.
  - (E): "If k ≥ 3, dim_K G^ord_k(Γ1(N), K) depends only on k mod p − 1 ([LFE] 7.2)."
  - Theorem 3.3 covers k ≥ 2. He adds that a version "for Γ0(N) and also for cusp forms ... is valid for all weights
    k ≥ 2" and points to [GME] Chapter 3, Theorem 3.2.17. GME was not read [unverified].
  - What this gives [derived]: V_ord[χ] is Λ-free. So for **every** continuous κ inducing χ, including z ↦ z^{−2},
    the ordinary Katz forms of weight κ have the same rank s(χ) as at classical k ≥ 3.
- **Calegari, AWS notes, 2.2.2 Theorem (Hida)**, "a consequence of results of Hida" [read].
  - Here M_k(Γ, R, 0) means p-adic (growth r = 0) forms of any integer weight k. His §2.3 uses weight 0 with p = 13.
  - "The operator e_p extends to an idempotent on M_k(Γ, R, 0)."
  - "(1) e_p M_k(Γ, R, 0) is finite dimensional, and the dimension depends only on k mod p − 1."
  - "(2) If k > 1, then e_p M_k(Γ, R, 0) ⊆ M_k(Γ0(p), R, 0) is spanned by classical modular forms."
  - "(3) The minimal polynomial of U_p on e_p M_k(Γ, R/p^n, 0) only depends on k mod p^{n−1}(p − 1)."
- **Hida, "Iwasawa modules attached to congruences of cusp forms", Ann. Sci. ENS (4) 19 (1986) 231–273**.
  - Numdam copy: https://www.numdam.org/item/ASENS_1986_4_19_2_231_0. I read it through poor OCR, so the wording is
    not quoted.
  - Theorem 3.1: the ordinary universal Hecke algebras are free of finite rank over Λ. Their rank equals the
    classical ordinary rank for k ≥ 3 with k ≡ a (mod p−1).
  - Corollary 3.2: control at classical weights only (k ≥ j(a) ∈ [3, p+1]; k ≥ 2 for cusp forms).
- **Coleman, "P-adic Banach spaces and families of modular forms", Invent. Math. 127 (1997) 417–479** [read].
  - Copy: https://wstein.org/people/coleman/papers/p-adic_banach_spaces.pdf.
  - Theorem B3.2: "There is a unique rigid analytic function P(s; T) = P_N(s; T)" with
    P(k; T) = det(1 − TU(k)|M_k(v)). This holds for k ∈ ℤ and rational v from 0 to p/(p+1); the inequality signs are
    garbled in my copy.
  - On the coefficients f_n(s): explicit formulas "imply that they are Iwasawa functions".
  - Proposition B5.8: every form of slope α at weight k lies in a family ("The map from H to M_k(N; i)^α is a
    surjection").
  - The introduction says Hida "proved what may now be interpreted as the slope zero part of the aforementioned
    conjectures".
  - What this gives [derived]:

## 4. The Shimura correspondence for η^r, with Atkin–Lehner signs

- **Yang, "Modular forms of half-integral weights on SL(2,Z)", Nagoya Math. J. 215 (2014) 1–66** [read].
  - DOI 10.1215/00277630-2684452. I read the TeX of arXiv:1110.1810 v1 (Oct 2011); the journal text was not compared.
  - Theorem 1 hypotheses: "Let r be an integer satisfying (r,6)=1 and 0<r<24 and s be a nonnegative even integer."
    The space is S_{r,s} = {η(24τ)^r f(24τ) : f ∈ M_s(1)} ⊂ S_{r/2+s}(576, (12/·)).
  - Theorem 1 statement: "Then the Shimura correspondence yields an isomorphism" between S_{r,s} and
    S^new_{r+2s−1}(6, −(8/r), −(12/r)) ⊗ (12/·), "as Hecke modules."
  - Theorem 2, case 3 | r: "Let r be an odd integer satisfying 0<r<8 and s be a nonnegative even integer."
    S_{3r,s} = {η(8τ)^{3r} f(8τ)} ⊂ S_{3r/2+s}(64, (−4/·)) is ≃ S^new_{3r+2s−1}(2, −(8/r)) ⊗ (−4/·). Its proof:
    "similar, but much simpler, and will be omitted."
  - So level 2 occurs exactly when 3 | r (write the exponent as 3r with r odd, 0 < r < 8); otherwise the level is 6.
    For the partition tower r ≡ −ℓ (mod 24), which is prime to 6, so only Theorem 1 applies [derived].
  - Notation: the newform space is of "weight 2k on Γ0(6)", "eigenfunctions with eigenvalues ϵ2 and ϵ3 for
    Atkin-Lehner involutions W2 and W3". The twist: "the notation ⊗(12/·) means the twist by the quadratic character
    (12/·)".
  - Corollary: "The multiplicity-one property holds for the spaces S_{r,s}".
  - Remark: "S^new_{2k}(6,ϵ2,ϵ3)⊗(12/·) is contained in S^new_{2k}(144,−,−), regardless of whether ϵ2,ϵ3 are 1 or −1".
    Similarly S^new_{2k}(2,ϵ2)⊗(−4/·) lies in S^new_{2k}(16,−).
  - Hecke operators:
    - T_{n²} is defined only for (n,6)=1 on the η-type spaces.
    - For p ∤ 6N, T_{p²} sends a_f(n) to a_f(p²n) + (12/p)((−1)^k n/p) p^{k−1} a_f(n) + p^{2k−1} a_f(n/p²). The
      half-integral weight is k + 1/2 = r/2 + s.
  - **The twist in the Hecke relation**, §"Comparison of traces":
    - It proves tr(T_{n²}|S_{r,s}(1)) = (12/n)·tr(T_n|S^new_{2k}(6, −(8/r), −(12/r))) for n prime to 6.
    - It adds: "Here we only work out the cases n ≡ 1 mod 24 and n ≡ 11 mod 24 and omit the proof for the other
      cases."
  - Method: "since the Hecke modules involved are all semisimple, to prove the theorems, it suffices to show that
    the traces coincide".
  - The introduction recalls Shimura's correspondence: weight k+1/2 with character χ goes to weight 2k with character
    χ² and "shares the same eigenvalues"; Niwa proved the level can be 2N.
  - The introduction also recalls Guo–Ono: G_ℓ = q + (8/r)2^{(ℓ−5)/2}q² + (12/r)3^{(ℓ−5)/2}q³ + ⋯. For
    13 ≤ ℓ ≤ 31, "the image of g_ℓ(τ) under the Shimura correspondence is G_ℓ⊗(12/·)".
- **Yang, "Congruences of the partition function", IMRN 2011, no. 14, 3261–3288** [read].
  - DOI 10.1093/imrn/rnq194. I read the TeX of arXiv:0904.2530 (Apr 2009).
  - Theorem 2 (FKO's "Theorem 2 of [39]"): "Let r be an odd integer with 0<r<24. Let s be a non-negative even
    integer."
  - It says S_{r,s} "is an invariant subspace of S_{s+r/2}(Γ0(576),χ12) under the action of the Hecke algebra".
    That is, f|T_{ℓ²} ∈ S_{r,s} "for all primes ℓ≠2,3".
  - Citing Guo–Ono, Prop. 6: the image is G_m ⊗ χ12, with G_m "the unique normalized newform of weight m−3 on
    Γ0(6)". Its eigenvalues for W2 and W3 "are −(2/m) and −(3/m), respectively" (for 13 ≤ m ≤ 31).
  - Remark: dim S_{r_m,(m−r_m−2)/2} = ⌊m/12⌋ − ⌊m/24⌋, where 0 < r_m < 24 and m ≡ −r_m (mod 24).
  - Corollary (Chua's conjecture): F_{m,j} ≡ η(24τ)^{r_{m,j}} φ_{m,j}(24τ) mod m.
    - r_{m,j} = r_m for odd j and 23 for even j.
    - The weight of φ is (m − r_m − 2)/2 for odd j and m − 13 for even j.
  - Corollary (recursion): U_{ℓ²}^k on a basis of S_{r,s} is A_k f + B_k g + C_k f|V_{ℓ²}, where
    (A_k; A_{k−1}) = [[A, −ℓ^{r+2s−2}I], [I, 0]]^k (I; 0) and A is the T_{ℓ²}-matrix. So in classical weight, U(ℓ²)
    runs through the Hecke polynomial X² − AX + ℓ^{r+2s−2}.
  - "Observe that the Hecke operator T_{m²} is the same as the operator U_{m²} modulo m."
- **Consistency with our claim** [derived; all from the quotes above]:
  - Take r ≡ −ℓ (mod 24) and s = (ℓ−r−2)/2. The characters (8/·) and (12/·) are even, and (8/·) depends on n mod 8.
    So −(8/r) = −(2|ℓ) and −(12/r) = −(3|ℓ).
  - So Yang's space is exactly S^new_{ℓ−3}(6, −(2|ℓ), −(3|ℓ)): the f in our claim.
  - The even-b tower has r = 23, with signs (−1, −1).
  - R_ℓ = ⌊(ℓ+12)/24⌋ = ⌊ℓ/12⌋ − ⌊ℓ/24⌋ (write ℓ = 24a + b to check). So the FKO/BW rank bound equals the number of
    such f.
  - (12/ℓ) = (3|ℓ) for primes ℓ ≥ 5.
  - Calegari's U2 = 2^{−2}, U3 = 3^{−2} at weight −2 means W2 = W3 = −1, using a_p = −w_p p^{k/2−1} for p ‖ N. This
    matches r = 23.
  - Moving to the classical member of weight ℓ−3 multiplies 2^{k/2−1} by 2^{(ℓ−1)/2} ≡ (2|ℓ) (mod ℓ). That gives
    w2 = −(2|ℓ), and likewise w3 = −(3|ℓ).
  - Calegari's 4.7.1 (p = 5, "w2 = w3 = +1") fits this labelling by the classical members.

**Gap for our use:**
- Yang's results are classical only: holomorphic, weight r/2 + s > 0, s ≥ 0. Weight −1/2 (η^{−1}) and
  overconvergent or ordinary forms are not covered.
- The Hecke dictionary uses T_{n²} with (n,6)=1 only. U at 2 and 3 are not in it, and the W-signs live only on the
  integral side.
- In the arXiv text the proof is incomplete as written: residues of n mod 24 other than 1 and 11 are omitted, and so
  is the whole proof of Theorem 2.

## Gaps a proof still has to close
1. **Calegari's Lemma 4.6.4 has no proof in print** (as far as found). It places the weight −1/2 η-type eigen-systems
   in S†_{−2}(Γ0(6)) ⊗ χ12 with U2 = 2^{−2}, U3 = 3^{−2}. An obvious route would use Yang's classical isomorphisms at
   the classical weights of both components, plus density of classical points in Ramsey's eigencurve. I found it
   written nowhere.
2. **Multiplicity.**
   - Ramsey's Sh is a bijection of eigen-systems onto a union of components, not a Hecke-module isomorphism.
   - Two dimensions should be equal: the ordinary half-integral space at weight −1/2 (each parity), and the number of
     ordinary f. Nothing published gives this. Yang's multiplicity one is classical only.
3. **Cuspidality.** Ramsey's lift is cuspidal. η(24z)^{−1} has poles: FKO's appendix gets only M†, and Calegari
   asserts S† without proof.
4. **The limit may not be the whole ordinary part.** FKO Theorem 7.1 gives only "⊆". BW find
   r_607(m) = R_607 − 1 for all m. So at ℓ = 607 one eigen-system appears to be missing from the tower, unless BW's
   rank notion differs. This should be checked against our "f runs over all" claim at ℓ = 607.
5. **Hida control at κ = −2, tame level 6.** It must be assembled: Hida's Theorem 3.1 (Katz forms, all κ), plus
   ordinary ⇒ overconvergent (attributed to Hida; exact theorem not located) or Coleman's P(k, T). Nothing
   comparable was found for half-integral weight. Hida's 1995 Λ-adic half-integral paper might have it [unverified].
6. **u_f(−2) must be well defined.** It needs f to be ℓ-ordinary and the Hida component through f to give a single
   value at κ = −2. BW's condition d_ℓ = 0 (true for all ℓ ≤ 1300) is the mod-ℓ invertibility of the step operator.
   That it amounts to ordinarity of all these f is my reading, not a published statement [unverified].
7. **Twist bookkeeping.** Each step is stated somewhere:
   - The U(ℓ²)-eigenvalue equals the U_ℓ-eigenvalue of the lift (Ramsey; no twist).
   - The lift is a level-6 family twisted by χ12 (Calegari 4.6.4; classically Yang).
   - So the factor is χ12(ℓ) = (3|ℓ).

   The overconvergent twist step rests only on Calegari 4.6.4.
8. **Versions.** These were read in preprint or arXiv form and not compared with the journals: FKO (preprint),
   Yang 2014 (arXiv v1), Ramsey (arXiv), Hida 2005 (author's notes).
