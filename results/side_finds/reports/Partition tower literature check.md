# Prior work frames the tower, not its numbers

**Correction, 20 Nov 2026.** This report first said no digit of the constant for 31 was in print. Shyam Narayanan's 2019 Harvard senior thesis (§4.8) gives the two-level multiplier −1 modulo 31, which is the first digit 30. His §4.3 to §4.7 give the first digits for 13 to 29 as well. The table below is corrected.

None of our eight findings is fully in print, and two are wholly new. The closest prior work is Frank Calegari's 2013 Arizona Winter School lecture notes. They already treat the partition tower as an object of weight −2 on level 6, carrying the same sign (3|ℓ) that we use. For ℓ = 5 they print 13 base-5 digits of our constant (quoted), and all 13 match ours (derived). Elsewhere, print holds only first digits for 7, 11, 17, 19 and 23, two digits for 13, three for 29, and nothing for 31. Our first-digit rule (C3) follows in a few lines from published lemmas, yet nobody writes it down; our multipliers along other primes (C4) are in print to the first digit only. Not found anywhere: our formula for the constants and its checks for 7 to 31; every deeper digit and every deeper multiplier; the slopes and weight patterns for 7 and 11; the twin constants for 37 and up; and the congruences at Q = 4049 and 8941 (ℓ = 43) and Q = 2999 (ℓ = 37) (computed by us). PARI's wrong partition number matches no known bug, and FLINT's certified value shows that PARI 2.15.4 was wrong and sympy right. Wherever print and our numbers overlap, they agree, once one misprint in Boylan–Webb is set right.

## Six findings are half-known; two are not in print at all

This report checks eight findings against the published record. It rests on four sets of research notes written on 12 Nov 2026, and on three results the project computed afterwards. Every number carries a label. **(quoted)** means copied from a source. **(computed by us)** means it comes from the project's own runs; the link goes to the output file. **(derived)** means short arithmetic, done in the notes or for this report, on quoted or computed numbers.

A few words recur. The **tower** is the partition numbers p(N) at those N for which 24N − 1 is divisible by ℓ, then by ℓ², then by ℓ³, and so on. "Level b" means ℓ^b divides 24N − 1. H_b is the level-b list divided by the power of ℓ that the old laws of Ramanujan, Watson and Atkin already guarantee. Our **constant** α_ℓ turns level b into level b + 2: H_(b+2) agrees with α_ℓ·H_b, to more digits the deeper one goes. **Digits** are base-ℓ digits, lowest first. Two numbers agree to k digits when they leave the same remainder on division by ℓ^k. A **newform of weight k on level 6** is a power series with a strong symmetry, with one coefficient a_Q for each prime Q. A **family** is a chain of such forms whose weights step by multiples of ℓ − 1 and whose coefficients change smoothly in the base-ℓ sense. Such a chain can be followed to weight −2, where no ordinary form exists. The **slope** is how many times ℓ divides a_ℓ (the "U_ℓ eigenvalue"). Slope 0 makes a Hida family; a higher slope makes a Coleman family. The **Shimura lift** is the standard bridge from half-integral weight, where the partition generating function lives (weight −1/2), to whole weight (here −2). A **multiplier along Q** links partition numbers at N and at Q²·N; the papers call it a Hecke eigenvalue. Finally, (3|ℓ) is +1 when 3 is a square modulo ℓ and −1 when it is not. The papers write the same sign as a "twist by χ12", because χ12(ℓ) = (3|ℓ) for every odd ℓ (derived).

## Print holds at most 13 digits of any constant (C1)

**C1 is partly known: the constants' existence is old, but print holds at most 13 digits of any of them.** For ℓ = 13, Atkin and O'Brien proved in 1967 that for every α a constant K_α links levels α and α + 2 modulo 13^α. Garvan and Shomanov restate this as their Theorem 1.1 ([Garvan–Shomanov, arXiv 2504.10824 (2025); Adv. Math. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10824)). Folsom, Kent and Ono (FKO) proved the same for every prime from 5 to 31, and added "If ℓ ∈ {5, 7, 11}, then A_ℓ(b1, b2, m) = 0" ([FKO, Adv. Math. 229 (2012), Thm 1.1](https://uva.theopenscholar.com/files/ken-ono/files/133_8.pdf)). Their constant is 0 there only because they do not divide out the old laws' powers of ℓ. So **our constants for 5, 7 and 11 lie outside their frame**, while for 13 to 31 their A_ℓ(b, b + 2, m) is our α_ℓ modulo ℓ^m (derived). Boylan and Webb later computed the limiting spaces modulo ℓ for every prime from 13 to 1300, modulo ℓ² up to 89, modulo ℓ³ up to 29, and modulo 13⁴. Yet they **printed constants only for 29** ([Boylan–Webb, Trans. AMS 365 (2013), §6.1](https://people.math.sc.edu/boylan/reprints/partition.pdf)).

The table sets our digits against every digit found in print. Bold digits are printed somewhere; the rest were not found. For 5, 7 and 11 our digits come from exact partition numbers. For 13 to 31 they come from the weight −2 route of C2, and the third column says how many of them exact partition numbers confirm.

Here is how the bold digits come out of print. **Calegari prints λ1 = 4·5² + 2·5³ + 3·5⁴ + 5⁵ + 2·5⁷ + 3·5⁹ + 5¹¹ + 5¹² + 5¹⁴ + ⋯ (quoted).** λ1 is the number that carries the 5-tower two levels at a time. Dividing it by 5², the power the old law adds per two levels, gives the 13 bold digits (derived). Watson's formula for 7 carries a factor 5^⌊j/2⌋ and Atkin's for 11 a factor 4^(3(j−1)/2) (quoted, from Jameson). So two levels multiply by 5, and by 4³ = 64 ≡ 9 (mod 11) (derived). For 13, Ono's §4 has a factor 6 per two levels, in congruences that "appear in Ramanujan's unpublished manuscript on τ(n) and p(n)" (quoted). FKO give "A₁₃(2, 4, 2) = 45" (quoted), and 45 = 6 + 3·13 (derived). For 17, Ono's two one-level factors 7 and 13 (quoted) multiply to 91 ≡ 6 (mod 17) (derived). For 19 and 23 his two-level factors are 10 and 5 (quoted). OEIS has no sequence holding the first 14 digits of our 5 constant ([search](https://oeis.org/search?q=4%2C2%2C3%2C1%2C0%2C2%2C0%2C3%2C0%2C1%2C1%2C0%2C1%2C4&fmt=short)) or the first 13 of our 7 constant ([search](https://oeis.org/search?q=5%2C6%2C1%2C1%2C2%2C1%2C3%2C1%2C0%2C6%2C5%2C3%2C1&fmt=short)).

**The 29 row is the one place where print runs ahead of our partition numbers, and it holds a misprint.** Boylan and Webb print three relations, with constants 10 (mod 29), 329 (mod 29²) and 14706 (mod 29³) (quoted). The first two run from the deeper level back to the shallower one, so they give the inverse of our constant. Indeed 10·3 ≡ 1 (mod 29) and 329·409 ≡ 1 (mod 29²), with 409 = 3 + 14·29 (derived). The third is not an inverse: **14706 = 3 + 14·29 + 17·29² is our constant itself**, third digit included, as our weight −2 route gives it (computed by us). 14706 leaves remainder 3 on division by 29, while 10 and 329 both leave 10 (derived). So the third display runs the opposite way from the first two. It reads "p(29⁵n + 19308) ≡ 14706 p(29⁷n + 13656152078) (mod 29³)" (quoted; checked in the PDF itself). Its offsets are off as well. The printed 806 and 19308, given for the progressions with 29³ and 29⁵, are the starting points of levels 2 and 3; levels 3 and 5 start at 19308 and 16237993 (derived). Our exact partition numbers had settled only the digits 3 and 14 (computed by us). So the printed 14706, read the right way round, is an outside check on the third digit that the forms predict.

The verdict has limits. Atkin and O'Brien's 1967 paper and Atkin's 1968 paper could not be read. FKO report that Atkin claimed a proof for 17 "modulo 17, 17², and 17³" (quoted), so the first three digits for 17 may stand there. Calegari says only that "one may numerically compute" λ1. He thanks David Loeffler "for making available some of his previous computations" (quoted), and those were never published.

## Calegari's notes hold the frame; the formula and its checks are ours (C2–C4)

**C2 is partly known.** The whole picture behind it is in Calegari's notes, stated for every prime above 3. By Ramsey's ℓ-adic ("overconvergent") Shimura lift, the weight −1/2 eigenform behind the tower matches an eigenform of weight −2, and "after twisting, this form lies in Γ0(6)" ([Calegari, "Congruences between modular forms", AWS 2013 notes, §4.6.3, p. 46](https://swc-math.github.io/aws/2013/2013CalegariLectureNotes.pdf)). Lemma 4.6.4 states: "The image of the Shimura correspondence from S_{−1/2}(1, r) lies in S†_{−2}(Γ0(6), r) ⊗ χ12" (quoted). The eigencurve, the space that holds all such families, then gives "a classical form of weight k ≡ −2 mod p − 1" (quoted). The sign (3|ℓ) in our formula is his χ12 (derived). His λ1 agrees with our α5 times 5² in all 13 printed digits (derived), and his Lemma 4.6.4 makes it (3|5)·u(−2), with (3|5) = −1 (derived). So for ℓ = 5, C2 is in print in substance.

What the notes lack is the formula as a statement, the route to it, and numbers for any prime but 5. Nobody writes α_ℓ = (3|ℓ)·u(−2)/ℓ^s. Nobody takes the family through the classical level-6 newform of weight ℓ − 3, computes its classical members at weights spaced ℓ − 1 apart (or (ℓ − 1)ℓ apart for 5, 7 and 11), and runs the series back to weight −2. Calegari uses classical weights only inside a proof by contradiction, to rule out slopes between 0 and 1. FKO's appendix, by Ramsey with the proof due to Calegari, shows only that the limit is the slope-0 part, and that rank 1 gives "a single nonzero Hecke eigenform" (quoted). The eigenvalue is never named ([FKO 2012, appendix, Thm 7.1](https://uva.theopenscholar.com/files/ken-ono/files/133_8.pdf)). The nearest precedent for the method is Guerzhoy's use of Hida's control theorem for the j-function at weight 0. It does not touch partitions ([Guerzhoy, Math. Res. Lett. 13 (2006)](http://www.math.hawaii.edu/~pavel/publications/atkin.pdf)). A weight −2 form on Γ0(6) also appears in the Bruinier–Ono exact formula for p(n), but that link is not ℓ-adic ([Bruinier–Folsom–Kent–Ono survey](https://uva.theopenscholar.com/files/ken-ono/files/143_8.pdf)). Sweeps of the works citing FKO found 34 in OpenAlex and 47 in Semantic Scholar (quoted counts). None ties FKO's constants to a family or to weight −2 ([OpenAlex list](https://api.openalex.org/works?filter=cites:W2049683464&per-page=50&select=id,display_name,publication_year,doi&sort=publication_year:desc)). A specialist would read C2 as what the Calegari–Ramsey picture predicts. What is new is the explicit formula, a way to compute it from classical forms, and the numbers that confirm it.

Those numbers are strong. **For every ℓ from 13 to 31, the family's value at weight −2 agrees with every digit our exact partition numbers settle: 8 of 8 for 13, 5 of 5 for 17 and 19, 4 of 4 for 23, 2 of 2 for 29 and 31. For 5, 7 and 11 it agrees on 19, 13 and 4 digits** (computed by us: [13 to 31](../partitions_hida_digits.txt), [13 deeper](../partitions_hida_check.txt), [5 and 11](../partitions_hida_laws.txt), [7](../partitions_hida_seven_14.txt)). The match is selective. At weight 154, only one of the 23 newforms that can be read 13-adically gives 45 modulo 13²: the member of f's Hida family (computed by us, [run](../partitions_hida.txt)). For 29, the route's third digit, 17, is the one Boylan–Webb's 14706 confirms (derived).

**C3 is partly known: implicit in print, never stated.** The form f is named in print. For 13 ≤ m ≤ 31 the Shimura image is "G_m ⊗ χ_12, where G_m is the unique normalized newform of weight m−3 on Γ_0(6) whose eigenvalues for the Atkin-Lehner involutions W_2 and W_3 are −(2/m) and −(3/m)" (quoted from [Yang, IMRN 2011](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0904.2530), citing Guo–Ono 1999, Prop. 6). Ahlgren, Allen and Tang (AAT) place every Shimura lift of the mod-ℓ partition form in that space, for every ℓ ≥ 5 ([AAT, Trans. AMS Ser. B 9 (2022), (2.8)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09481)). Their Lemma 5.1 gives "f|T(Q²) ≡ (12/Q) λ_Q f (mod ℓ)" (quoted). Ahlgren, Andersen and Dicks prove the twisted match "for each prime p ≥ 5", which includes ℓ itself ([AAD, Adv. Math. 447 (2024), Thm 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07438)). Yang notes that "the Hecke operator T_{m²} is the same as the operator U_{m²} modulo m" (quoted). Going up two levels is, in the papers' terms, the operator U(ℓ²), and our constant is its eigenvalue. So these pieces give C3 in three lines (derived). The first digits 6, 6, 10 and 5 for 13, 17, 19 and 23 are printed in Ono 2000, verified by a finite check (Sturm's theorem), with no word about a_ℓ(f) (quoted). The analogue for the j-function is classical (Serre; quoted in Guerzhoy 2006). **New: the statement, and the check that the measured first digit equals (3|ℓ)·a_ℓ(f) modulo ℓ for each of 13, 17, 19, 23, 29 and 31** (computed by us, [run](../partitions_split.txt)).

**C4 is partly known: stated to the first digit, implicit beyond it.** That multipliers along another prime exist modulo every power ℓ^m is FKO's Corollary 1.4 for 5 ≤ ℓ ≤ 31; no values are given. For 13, Garvan and Shomanov proved Atkin and O'Brien's conjecture for all powers of 13, again with no values ([Garvan–Shomanov, Cor. 1.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10824)). The first digit is AAT's Lemma 5.1: (12|Q) times the Q-th coefficient of the level-6 form. For 7 and 11, Jameson gives it through two elliptic curves, "E7 (which has conductor 42)" and E11 "(which has conductor 726)" (quoted, [Jameson, Thm 3.3](https://web.math.utk.edu/~mjameso2/research/03.pdf)). Past the first digit, the match with a weight −2 member follows from the fact that Ramsey's lift respects the multipliers along every Q ([Ramsey, IMRN 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.3255)). Calegari notes only that his φ1 "is an eigenform for the Hecke operators T_{ℓ²} with ℓ ≠ 5" (quoted). No source computes a multiplier past the first digit, or compares one with a weight −2 member. One unread source matters here. FKO say "Atkin [7] found such congruences modulo 13², 17³, 19², 23⁶ [sic], 29, and 31" (quoted). So Atkin's 1968 paper may hold some multipliers to two or three digits; what link to forms it draws, if any, is unknown.

**Our numbers make the implicit fact visible.** For 13, 17 and 19 at Q = 5, 7 and 11, the multiplier measured at level b shares about b digits with the weight −2 member, but only one with the classical form f (computed by us, [run](../partitions_hida_steps.txt)). For 5 the agreement reaches 12 digits; for 7, 12 digits at level 9; for 11, up to 5 digits at level 7 (computed by us: [5](../partitions_hida_law_steps_5.txt), [7](../partitions_hida_seven_steps.txt), [11](../partitions_hida_law_steps_11.txt)). For 7 a middle layer shows. **Modulo 49, the multipliers follow Jameson's curve E7 with Q¹⁹ in place of her Q. The rule is right at 74 of 74 primes at level 2, 42 of 42 at level 3 and 21 of 21 at level 4, and wrong modulo 343** (computed by us: [mod 49](../partitions_curve49.txt), [mod 343](../partitions_curve343.txt)). Since Q¹⁹ ≡ Q (mod 7), Jameson's theorem is the first digit of this rule (derived). Its second digit was not found in print. Beyond it, the curve's rule stays at 2 digits while the weight −2 member keeps gaining (computed by us).

## Slope 2 for 5 is printed; 7 and 11 are left as an exercise (C5)

**C5 is partly known.** For ℓ = 5 it is stated outright. Calegari writes that the eigenvalues "have slope 2, 7, 9, 15, 19, 22, 27, 29, 36, 39, …". He adds: "The fact that φ1 has slope 2 corresponds to the congruence for the partition function modulo powers of 5" ([Calegari 2013, §4.7, p. 47](https://swc-math.github.io/aws/2013/2013CalegariLectureNotes.pdf)). **Our classical members match his list. At weight 198, the members of the tower's mod-5 system have slopes 2, 7, 9, 15, 19, 22, 27, 29** (computed by us, [run](../partitions_hida_laws.txt)). **These are the first eight of his ten printed slopes** (derived).

For 7 and 11 the same reading is set as homework. Exercise 4.7.2 asks the reader to "Explain these congruences in terms of the eigenvalue of U_p or U_{p²} of smallest slope" (quoted). The general principle is printed. FKO's appendix calls the vanishing of the slope-0 part "exactly the situation of Watson's and Atkin's generalization of Ramanujan's congruences" (quoted). Ono's 2026 paper derives the congruences from "the Watson–Atkin contraction", with U7²(M⁰7) ⊆ 7M⁰7 and U11(M⁰11) ⊆ 11M11 (quoted, [Ono, arXiv 2508.09608v4 (2026)](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2508.09608)). None of them names slope 1 for 7 or slope 2 for 11. Both follow at once from the old exponents: Watson's 7^(⌊b/2⌋+1) gains one 7 per two levels, and Atkin's 11^b gains two 11s (derived). Our families show them directly. For 7 the slope is 1 at every weight we computed, from 16 to 292; for 11 it is 2 at weights 108, 218 and 328 (computed by us: [7](../partitions_hida_seven.txt), [11](../partitions_hida_laws.txt)).

**The weight patterns were not found for these systems.** For 5, the slope is exactly 2 at weights ≡ 18, 2 and 6 (mod 20), and 3 at weights ≡ 10 and 14. For 11, the first digit repeats with period 110 in the weight and is symmetric about weight 68. For 7, (3|7)·a_7/7 rises by 1 modulo 7 with each weight step of 6, from 1 at weight 16 to 6 at weight 46 (all computed by us: [5 and 11](../partitions_hida_laws.txt), [7](../partitions_hida_seven.txt)). General theory exists. By Hida's theorem, the slope-0 picture modulo pⁿ depends only on the weight modulo p^(n−1)(p − 1) ([Calegari 2013, Thm 2.2.2](https://swc-math.github.io/aws/2013/2013CalegariLectureNotes.pdf)). Liu, Truong, Xiao and Zhao prove a form of the Gouvêa–Mazur conjecture for positive slope "in the reducible and strongly generic case" (quoted, [LTXZ, arXiv 2302.07697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07697)). Whether their conditions cover these level-6 systems was not checked. The closest numerical work on slopes, Loeffler's, is for weight 0 and level 1, and never mentions partitions ([Loeffler, IMRN 2007](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0701168)).

The mod-ℓ shadows of all three families are in print, though never as families. Lovejoy and Ono found the mod-5 multipliers, and Jameson the mod-7 and mod-11 ones ([Jameson](https://web.math.utk.edu/~mjameso2/research/03.pdf), restating Lovejoy–Ono on pp. 1–2). Jameson's forms sit in S16(Γ0(6)) and S28(Γ0(6)) (quoted). Since 16 ≡ −2 (mod 6) and 28 ≡ −2 (mod 10) (derived), they are the first-digit layer of the very families in C5, though she never says so. **New: slopes 1 and 2 for 7 and 11 as statements; the weight patterns; the classical members we used (weights 18 to 258 for 5, 40 to 292 for 7, 108 to 328 for 11); and the constants of 7 and 11 computed from them.**

## From 37 on, print stops at the first digit (C6–C7)

**C6 is partly known.** That one form no longer suffices from 37 on is in print. AAT write that "when ℓ=37, the relevant space is two-dimensional" ([AAT 2022, §1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.09481)). Yang writes that "The two eigenforms of S_{11,12} are defined over a certain real quadratic number field", and that their reductions modulo 37 are defined over F_37 ([Yang 2011, Example 6.2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0904.2530)). That means 37 splits in that field (derived). For 41, 43 and 47, only general formulas give the dimension 2. Yang's ⌊m/12⌋ − ⌊m/24⌋ and Boylan–Webb's rank bound ⌊(ℓ + 12)/24⌋ (quoted) both equal 2 there (derived). Boylan–Webb computed that the rank meets that bound for every prime up to 1297 except 607 (quoted). No paper names the fields. Our defining polynomials were not found in papers: y² − y − 7187088385710 for 37, y² − y − 62801230922892 for 41, y² − y − 3231324102 for 43, and y² − y − 118780096383848578140 for 47. Neither was the fact that 37, 41 and 43 split while 47 stays prime (all computed by us, [run](../partitions_split.txt)). The newform database LMFDB, which may list these fields, was left out of the search by instruction.

Along powers of ℓ, the rank-2 structure is printed in general form and for other primes. Boylan and Webb print three-term relations for ℓ = 53, modulo 53 and modulo 53², the second on levels 3, 5 and 7. They describe their period c_ℓ as "the order of a matrix in GL_{r_ℓ(1)}(Z/ℓZ)", with c_37 = 36 and c_41 = 10 (quoted, [Boylan–Webb 2013](https://people.math.sc.edu/boylan/reprints/partition.pdf)). Our law H_(b+4) ≡ T·H_(b+2) − N·H_b uses (T, N) = (12, 29) for 37, (13, 16) for 41 and (37, 14) for 43 (computed by us: [37 and 41](../partitions_twins.txt), [43](../partitions_twins_43.txt)). **The matrices with rows (T, −N) and (1, 0) have order 36 modulo 37 and order 10 modulo 41 (derived). These are exactly Boylan–Webb's printed periods.** For 43 the same rule predicts a period of 21 (derived), which they do not print. So their printed periods and our numbers agree. But the values of T and N were not found, nor their reading as the trace and norm of (3|ℓ)·a_ℓ over the twin pair. Neither were the twins' own constants: for 37, 15, 4, 4, 3, 1, … and 34, 30, 35, 25, 2, …; for 41, 23, 14, 7, 38, 12, … and 31, 11, 10, 38, 14, …; for 43, 31, 13, 8, 5, 40 and 6, 20, 10, 25, 3 (computed by us). With every partition number recomputed by FLINT, the law holds modulo ℓ at levels 1, 3, 5 and 2, 4, 6, and modulo ℓ² at levels 3, 5, 7, for 37, 41 and 43 (computed by us, [run](../partitions_flint_arbiter.txt)). That is where Boylan–Webb's bound b_ℓ(2) ≤ 3 says the second digit should settle (derived).

Along other primes Q, the law exists in matrix form. Yang's Corollary 6 and Theorem 7 give the exact recursion for repeated steps along Q on the whole two-dimensional space. They use a block matrix whose corner holds −Q^(m−4) (quoted), which is −Q⁻³ modulo m (derived): the same term as in our law. His Remark 5.1 gives one 2×2 matrix per eigenform (quoted). The scalar trace-and-norm form we use was not found. Its first digit is in print, though. **Yang's table of the two eigenvalues modulo 37 has (1, 32), (33, 10), (22, 0) and (7, 6) at Q = 5, 7, 11 and 13 (quoted). These are exactly our pairs** (computed by us, [run](../partitions_split.txt); compared for this report). Our law fits 48 of 48 chains for each of 37, 41, 43 and 47. A single constant fits only 9, 6 and 0 of 48 for 37, 41 and 43 (computed by us).

**C7 was not found.** For ℓ = 37 and Q below 10,000, AAT report three "accidents": "we have a congruence (1.6) when Q=6599, 7541, and 9547" (quoted). They print the sign for 6599 (−1) and 7541 (+1) only. Our search found exactly these three, with sign −1 at 9547 (computed by us). That sign agrees with AAT's Theorem 1.2 rule, since 9547 ≡ 1 (mod 37) and 9547 ≡ 3 (mod 4) (derived). So this part is a rediscovery, plus one unprinted sign. **For 43 we found Q = 4049 and Q = 8941, both with sign −1, and none for 41 or 47 below 10,000** (computed by us). Neither 4049 nor 8941 occurs in AAT, in 17 other full texts searched, or in OEIS. Both lie outside AAT's provable families Q ≡ 1 and Q ≡ −2 (mod ℓ), since 4049 ≡ 7 and 8941 ≡ −3 (mod 43) (derived). So they are accidents in Atkin's sense. The published searches cover only 13 ≤ ℓ ≤ 31: Weaver's 76,065 congruences and Johansson's "slightly more than 2.2 × 10^10" (quoted, [Johansson, LMS J. Comput. Math. 15 (2012)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1205.5991)). No published search covers 41, 43 or 47 at all. The only congruences modulo a prime ≥ 37 at a named Q found in print are AAT's three, and a family of Yang's for 37 with Q ≤ 61. Yang calls his "the first example in literature where a congruence (1) modulo a prime m ≥ 37 is explicitly given" (quoted). So 4049 and 8941 appear to be the first named congruences of this kind modulo 43. FLINT confirms both members of each class, and the controls (computed by us, [run](../partitions_flint_arbiter.txt)).

At Q = 2999, both of 37's multipliers vanish (computed by us). This gives p(N) ≡ 0 (mod 37) whenever 24N − 1 = 37·2999³·m with 2999 not dividing m. The rule behind it is printed. When the multiplier matrix vanishes modulo m, p((m^j Q³ n + 1)/24) ≡ 0 (mod m); Yang calls this "the congruence appearing in Ono's theorem" ([Yang 2011, Remark 3.2 and Thm 7](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0904.2530)). Yang's table has no Q ≤ 61 where both vanish (quoted), which fits a first such prime far higher. The instance at 2999 was not found. And 2999 ≡ 2 (mod 37) (derived), so it is not one of Ahlgren and Ono's primes Q ≡ −1 (mod 24ℓ). **FLINT gives p(540584055355) ≡ 0 (mod 37), where 24·540584055355 − 1 = 37·2999³·13** (computed by us; the factorization derived). Eight partition numbers one step down confirm that both multipliers vanish (computed by us, [run](../partitions_split_2999.txt)). One naming caution: our "Type II" (both multipliers 0) is not AAT's "Atkin II". Theirs is a different family, with Q ≡ −2 (mod ℓ) ([AAT 2022, (1.7)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.09481)).

## PARI's wrong value is an unreported bug, and FLINT settles it (C10)

**C10 was not found: no such bug is on record.** PARI 2.15.4's numbpart, called through cypari2 under WSL, gave p(56628877314) ≡ 23 (mod 43) and ≡ 62975 (mod 43³), in two separate runs. sympy 1.14 gave 36 and 63332 (computed by us, [run](../partitions_twins_43_sympy.txt)). **FLINT (python-flint 0.9.0) gives 36 and 63332. So PARI was wrong and sympy right** (computed by us, [run](../partitions_flint_arbiter.txt)). FLINT is the program with a guarantee. "The result is exact: the formula is evaluated in ball arithmetic with a proved bound for the truncation error", and the function "throws if the final ball does not determine a unique integer" (quoted, [FLINT documentation](https://flintlib.org/doc/partitions/index.html); see also [Johansson's page](https://fredrikj.net/math/hrr.html)). FLINT agrees with PARI on all 15 other law members and controls for 37 and 43 (computed by us), so the fault does not strike every large n. It mattered. With PARI's value, 43's three-term law failed even modulo 43 at levels 3, 5, 7. With the true value it holds modulo 43², like 37's and 41's (computed by us).

PARI's bug tracker holds 2,302 reports (derived, by counting the list). Only one is a wrong-value report for numbpart: bug #557 of 2007, "numbpart(n) returns incorrect results for n>=52602". It was off by one, because the sum was evaluated "starting from the *largest* terms", and it was fixed in version 2.4.2 (quoted, [bug #557](https://pari.math.u-bordeaux.fr/cgi-bin/bugreport.cgi?bug=557)). No changelog from 2.5 to 2.19.0 lists a numbpart fix; 2.19.0 lists only "speed up numbpart" ([2.19.0 changelog](https://pari.math.u-bordeaux.fr/pub/pari/unix/pari-2.19.0.changelog)). cypari2's tracker has no issue that mentions numbpart ([cypari2 issues](https://github.com/sagemath/cypari2/issues)). The known WSL fault in partition counts came from "long double" arithmetic in Sage's old code. PARI 2.15.4's source contains no long double at all (derived, 0 files found) ([Sage #29600](https://github.com/sagemath/sage/issues/29600), [WSL #830](https://github.com/microsoft/WSL/issues/830)).

The code explains little. Version 2.15.4 sums ⌊0.24√n + 5⌋ terms with 32 spare bits. It rounds without checking how close the sum lies to an integer (read from the source, [PARI 2.15.4](https://pari.math.u-bordeaux.fr/pub/pari/OLD/2.15/pari-2.15.4.tar.gz)). At this n that is 57,117 terms (derived). Rademacher's proven error bound does not certify that many terms: it gives about 1.3, where 0.5 is needed. Yet rough direct estimates put the truncation error near 0.15 and the rounding error near 10⁻⁴ (all derived, rough). PARI's own tests check numbpart only below 10⁴, plus three values up to 147,007 (quoted). The size of the error tells most. **The two values differ by at least 357** (derived, from 63332 − 62975). Every documented failure of this formula was off by one: PARI #557, and sympy #24267 and #26750 (quoted, [sympy #24267](https://github.com/sympy/sympy/issues/24267)). This is a different kind of fault. The sources cannot tell whether it lies in PARI's code at this n or in the particular build.

## Conclusion

The literature splits cleanly. Structure is published, much of it decades ago: that the constants exist, that they come from level-6 forms of weight ℓ − 3, that weight −2 is where they live, what they are to the first digit, and that a second form enters at 37. The ℓ-adic numbers past the first digit are almost absent. The only exceptions are Calegari's 13 digits for 5, FKO's 45 for 13 and Boylan–Webb's 14706 for 29. Our contribution is therefore quantitative, and it turns a picture into a procedure: from one classical newform, the formula yields every digit of every constant, and exact partition numbers check it independently. That every overlap with print agrees is strong outside support. In one place our numbers also correct print: Boylan–Webb's third ℓ = 29 display runs the wrong way, and its offsets are shifted.

"Not found" carries different weight for different claims. The searches could not read Atkin–O'Brien 1967, Atkin 1968, Weaver 2001, Chua 2003, Calegari's 2011 preprint on FKO, Johansson's offline tables, MathOverflow or LMFDB. Atkin 1968 is the main risk to the C1 and C4 verdicts for 13 to 31, since it reportedly holds congruences modulo 13², 17³ and 19². The C7 verdict is the firmest. Five independent descriptions agree that the large published searches stop at ℓ = 31, and none of the 18 full texts searched contains 4049, 8941 or 2999. The PARI episode adds a working lesson. A standard library returned a partition number wrong by at least 357 at n ≈ 5.7 × 10¹⁰, the error hid a true law, and only a certified value settled the question.
